## The answer

**Yes: a paid advanced Radar assistant is a viable product hypothesis worth
building and testing. There is not yet a demonstrated, repeat-purchase business.**
The best first offer is a managed research assistant for individual memecoin
traders and researchers: investigate a launch, challenge the claim that brought
you to it, preserve the evidence, and watch for the facts that change the answer.
Buyers purchase completed investigations and bounded monitoring with research
credits. A monthly allowance can follow demonstrated repeat use. An annual
unlimited AI promise should not.

Josh clarified the audience during this research: **degens, professional traders
and researchers**. The website operates from Tennessee; entity form and
incorporation were not established. He wants the widest lawful reach. The
recommendation therefore targets individual research workflows, not an
institutional compliance product, and treats global distribution as a separate
question from Tennessee operation.

The paid job is concrete: *“Here is the mint and the claim. Check the evidence I
would otherwise have to gather across several tools, show what remains unknown,
and tell me when something relevant changes.”* A user returns for the next mint,
the next disputed claim, or a change in a saved investigation. They need not
believe Radar predicts prices to value that work.

**Keep building toward an assistant that finds trading edge.** The current
0 bps measurement does not evaluate every future reasoning strategy. Give that
work a prospective measurement protocol and operator-funded research budget.
Charge for a useful research deliverable when one exists; change performance
claims only when the relevant evidence exists. Neither ambition nor billing
permission authorises a trade.

### Is an advanced AI trader a good business model?

**It could be an excellent product and potentially the more valuable one.**
A capable trader could provide a continuing reason to pay: better decisions,
less manual monitoring and useful execution within the customer's bounds. But
“advanced AI trader” names the product, not its business model. Subscription,
credits or a combination could fund either a trader or a research assistant.
Research credits are not inherently better than trading credits, and changing
the label establishes neither customer value nor a legal exemption.

There are two unproven propositions: that a proposed Radar trader delivers
enough value to retain customers, and that a proposed Radar researcher does.
The current deterministic instrument's 0 bps does not answer the first, and
competitors' pricing does not answer the second. Neither absence is proof of
impossibility. The case for researching first is that the existing dataset is
closer to a useful investigation workflow and its quality can be evaluated
without waiting for realised trading outcomes. That is a build-and-validation
argument, not evidence of a higher ultimate ceiling.

Nor must all trading software demonstrate superior returns before anyone can
pay for it. Users may pay for working tools and convenience, with honest claims
about those functions. Radar currently has no functioning customer trading
offer, however, and a future claim of better net trading results needs a test
of that actual service. Operator-funded discovery can run alongside a paid
research product; customers should receive useful work now rather than only
a promise of later profits.

The strongest long-term design may be **one assistant that researches,
monitors and eventually proposes trades**, with the same evidence record and
deterministic authorisation boundary. The first paid scope should expand when
its capabilities, demand and legal operating model justify it. For a future
trading service, prepaid reasoning must never become a dependency of revocation,
deterministic risk controls or management of existing obligations. Running out
of inference money must not strand an open position. That behaviour needs an
explicit execution design before such a service is offered.

### Josh's requirement: Radar must still be useful with a zero balance

**Owner requirement, clarified 2026-09-09:** good free tools and resources remain
available; completed paid research is retained; only additional paid research
halts when credits run out. This is stronger than “you can still self-host.”
The hosted free product must let someone complete useful work independently.

The following is the proposed product boundary, not a claim that all these
surfaces are already implemented:

| Available without a paid balance | What credits add |
|---|---|
| Standard mint fact sheets, recorded creator history, dated comparisons and clear coverage limits | An assistant investigates a particular claim, acquires additional evidence within budget and resolves follow-up questions |
| Deterministic cost/impact calculations for supported captured states, with assumptions and freshness visible | An assistant compares scenarios and produces an explained research case; the arithmetic itself remains usable free |
| The decision/refusal browser, source references and public research | Deeper managed acquisition and multi-step reasoning |
| Saved reports, citations, recorded watch history, bookmarks/notes and ordinary exports | New paid analysis, new captures beyond free collection, and explicitly priced ongoing watches |
| Corrections to Radar's reports and notices that a watch stopped or lost coverage | New customer-requested reassessment that goes beyond fixing Radar's error |

Credit exhaustion does not delete, lock, blur or unpublish purchased research.
It does not disable free functions. Each report keeps its observation date; a
stopped watch keeps its last known state and says that updates have paused.
Opening an old report, reading its already-generated explanation or exporting
it is not a new paid job. Asking the model a new question about it may be, and
the interface must distinguish those actions before charging.

Finished reports and legally retainable evidence should have durable account
access and ordinary export without a continuing subscription. Large raw captures
or third-party content may have separate, disclosed retention limits; preserve
the report, provenance and explicit expiry markers rather than pretending an
expired source can still be replayed. Legal deletion obligations still apply.

Free operation has a budget, so published fair-use limits are reasonable; they
must not be set so low that the free product is only a teaser. Measure whether a
zero-balance user can inspect a mint, calculate a supported scenario, inspect a
refusal and retrieve their past work. If those workflows fail, the proposed
pricing model has failed Josh's requirement even if paid conversion rises.

## Why pay when the code is free?

Running Radar's Apache-licensed software can remain free. Operating a dependable
research service means obtaining data, keeping historical observations, repairing
decoders, maintaining coverage, managing suppliers, and delivering answers while
the customer is elsewhere. A user can reproduce that operation; most would have
to spend time and accept operational responsibility to do so.

That is a legitimate service business, but **hosting alone is not a strong
advantage**. The defensible asset to build is an accumulated, reproducible record
of what was observable when, paired with a workflow that resolves a user's
question. Raw public chain data and model access are widely available. Data
licences still govern what Radar may store or redistribute; owning a decoder
does not give it ownership of every input.

The competitive baseline, checked 2026-09-08, is demanding:

- [Bubblemaps V2](https://wiki.bubblemaps.io/bubblemaps-v2/premium) offers
  real-time data, Magic Nodes and Time Travel free without restrictions.
  [Magic Nodes](https://wiki.bubblemaps.io/bubblemaps-v2/magic-nodes) already
  exposes indirect connections. A holder map with an AI explanation is a weak
  paid core.
- [Nansen Pro](https://academy.nansen.ai/en/help/articles/9412804-about-nansen-pro)
  advertises $69 monthly or $49/month billed annually, 2,000 agent prompts,
  portfolio tools and smart alerts. That establishes a priced alternative, not
  verified subscriber demand, profitability or renewal rates.
- [Replit's billing documentation](https://docs.replit.com/billing/ai-billing)
  describes usage credits spanning Agent and cloud services, including some
  third-party APIs. The useful analogy is paying for managed work and its
  resources. It does not establish that Radar needs a proprietary foundation
  model or that this audience will buy credits.

Radar must beat a practical baseline: a user with free chain tools, Bubblemaps,
Radar's own deterministic output and a general assistant. The measurable
advantage should be **less work to reach a correct, sourced answer**, plus
continuity when evidence changes. If the same result is equally easy without
Radar's paid workflow, there is no reason to refill a balance.

## The proposed product contract

Call the first offer **Radar Research**. The name matters less than these
behaviours:

1. A user supplies a mint, claim or saved case. A wallet connection is optional
   for research. The account buys service; it grants no trading authority.
2. The assistant resolves the subject and previews the investigation scope,
   maximum retail charge and stopping conditions. It may propose an additional
   lookup inside an already authorised budget. A larger scope needs a new cap.
3. It returns a case: answer, supporting and conflicting evidence, transaction
   references, observation times/slots, coverage limits and unresolved questions.
   An unsupported conclusion remains unsupported even if the budget is unused.
4. The customer can save it and choose named events, duration, cadence and a
   spend cap to watch. Notifications explain changed evidence. They are not
   instructions to buy, sell or hold.
5. The case remains readable/exportable after credits run out. New paid work
   pauses visibly. Errors in Radar's previous answer and monitoring outages are
   reported without charging the customer for the correction.

“Advanced” means choosing useful evidence, checking contradictions and deciding
when the evidence is insufficient. It is not a more confident writing style.
Read-only tool requests need their own deterministic scope/spend checks;
untrusted token/social content remains data. Extend the existing whole-clause
approach for externally checkable factual claims rather than assuming today's
free-form chat provides its guarantee. No research tool calls a signer.

## Capabilities, claims and collection costs

The six capabilities below are **proposals**, not shipped offers. Screenshot
sentences are illustrative product copy, not findings about a real token or
customer. Each would need its referenced capture before being shown as a fact.
Shared costs and the dollar assumptions are detailed in the next section.

### 1. Investigate this launch and the claim behind it

**Job:** “The community says this was a fair launch. Check that, and show the
costs at the size I entered.” Deliver a bounded case, not a safety score. Resolve
the mint, inspect successful launch transactions and recipient structure, join
recorded creator history, check fresh curve/fee state, and distinguish what the
claim would require from what was actually observed. Offer follow-up acquisition
only where it could change the answer.

**Data and build:** dossier, creator index, base-rate snapshot and curve formulas
exist. Customer case orchestration, persistent evidence references and a
user-entered size worksheet do not. The worksheet must distinguish computed
impact at the captured state from uncertain execution slippage, rent, priority
fees and failure costs. Stop at graduation if a supported route is unavailable.
Do not turn 0022's arithmetic into a current executable quote.

**Collection:** use the existing 60-call/20-second ceiling for an initial pass;
show `AtLeast`/partial history when it ends. At the raw-RPC rate below, 60 eligible
one-credit calls represent $0.00030 in marginal credits. That excludes the plan,
CPU, raw block size and model. A separately capped deeper pass might permit
1,000 raw calls; historical availability is not guaranteed by the budget.

**Claims:** facts found in successful transactions, dated population comparisons
and arithmetic under stated inputs. **Refuses:** “fair,” “safe,” “scam,” known
cabal identity, guaranteed exit, future price or appropriate personal position
size. Recipient counts do not establish independent buyers or Jito bundle
membership. Lack of detected coordination does not prove its absence.

**Screenshot:** “The launch block contains eleven recipient token accounts; the
claim that these were eleven independent buyers is not established.”

**Why buy again:** the next mint or disputed claim needs a new investigation.
The free dossier is the baseline; paid value must come from resolving the user's
specific question with additional evidence, not paraphrasing it.

### 2. Watch the facts behind a saved thesis

**Job:** “Watch these five launches until tomorrow. Tell me if the creator acts
again, the curve changes materially or a fact in this report is corrected.”
Deliver a quiet, bounded watch with an evidence diff, coverage status and expiry.
The user selects observable events; Radar explains changes rather than silently
deciding what the user should do with their holdings.

**Data and build:** launch records, creator index, captured curve state,
`measured_on`, typed fact kinds and the analyst log are usable foundations.
Watchlists, receipt-to-source dependencies, customer delivery and recovery after
outages are new. Monitoring creator history requires its own fresh collector;
a stale index cannot support an immediate alert. Holder/funder changes remain
unsupported until their collection exists.

**Collection:** a worked ceiling of ten mints, one one-credit read per mint per
minute, is 14,400 reads/day: $0.072/day or $2.16 per thirty days in marginal RPC
credits. This covers only that one state read, not arbitrary event detection.
Additional calls, retained bytes and model explanations are additive. Share
identical observations across users where licensing and privacy permit; run the
model on material changes rather than every poll. A one-minute poll is no
promise of one-minute alert delivery or protection before a sale.

**Claims:** a named observable fact changed between two captured states, or
coverage was interrupted. **Refuses:** “nothing bad happened” during a gap,
continuous protection, a working stop loss, or “your investment thesis is now
invalid” without distinguishing the user's premise from Radar's observation.

**Screenshot:** “The creator launched another token after your last check; here
is the transaction and the change to the history in your saved report.”

**Why buy again:** continuity while the user is away. An additional useful
behaviour is recalling affected receipts when a decoder or base rate is
corrected. Existing design work already proposes corrections; identifying each
private answer affected is an extension, not claimed as an entirely new idea.
Correcting Radar's mistakes is part of the original service, not paid retention.

### 3. Was that advertised win available after the call arrived?

**Job:** audit a caller's next set of calls prospectively. Record the full
submitted stream, Radar's receipt time, original timestamps and later edits.
Freeze example size, reaction delays and evaluation horizons before outcomes.
Report what could be observed after delivery, estimated transaction costs and
whether an exit could be measured. Compare that with the caller's advertised
chart window, keeping actual transactions separate from hypothetical results.

**This is the new proposal for Josh.** No equivalent prospective caller-audit
product was found in the inspected GOAL/design/research/ADR material. That is a
bounded search result, not knowledge of every idea the owner has considered.
Its distinctive question is access to the advertised opportunity after
communication delay, not whether a wallet or creator looks impressive.

**Data and build:** Radar's watermark discipline, cost research, launch/outcome
records and feature/edge machinery contribute. The call stream, authenticated
delivery timing and sufficiently granular subsequent state capture are new.
[0020](0020-the-exit-rule-question-cannot-be-answered-here.md) explains why coarse
checkpoint paths cannot establish intraperiod exits. Missing historical states
cannot be recovered by asking a model to infer them.

**Collection:** start with user-supplied or prospectively forwarded calls, so
there is no purchased social feed. For a twenty-call pilot, one read per minute
for six hours per call is 7,200 reads, $0.036 marginal raw credits, before launch
blocks, transaction histories, storage and inference. That cadence is only a
coarse descriptive pilot: it cannot support a sub-minute executability claim.
High-resolution collection requires measured bandwidth/retention and a separate
budget. If X is requested, 500 paid post reads add $2.50 at the checked tariff.

**Claims:** observable timing and hypothetical outcomes under a frozen protocol,
with missing/failed exits and all submitted calls in the denominator.
**Refuses:** actual follower profit, causal proof, completeness of a curated or
deleted feed, a guaranteed fill, or a recommendation to copy the caller. A user
upload's timestamp is asserted unless independently verified. Rename the result
“timing comparison” when executable states are unavailable; do not publish a
copyable-return number from prices alone.

**Screenshot:** “The advertised chart begins before this call reached Radar;
the after-delivery comparison includes the calls with no measurable exit.”

**Why buy again:** the next batch, caller or month. This could outrank watches
for professionals if paid trials show demand. Public rankings of identifiable
callers introduce additional accuracy/promotion risk; begin with private cases.

### 4. Explain what happened to my trade money

**Job:** a bounded execution-cost autopsy for a supplied wallet and period.
Reconcile supported transactions into proceeds, venue/network/priority fees,
identified tips, account rent movements and unexplained residuals. Separate
transfers, inventory still held and swaps; do not treat deposits as trading
profit or refundable rent as permanently lost.

**Data and build:** local decoding, transaction success semantics and Radar's
cost research help. Wallet pagination, owner resolution, balance reconciliation,
cross-route accounting and a customer report are new. Present-day chat does not
see the wallet's positions. A pump.fun-first implementation must show unsupported
venues explicitly; it cannot present a partial wallet as total PnL.

**Collection:** one wallet/declared period, initially capped at 10,000 eligible
raw reads ($0.05 marginal credits), plus local decoding, retained bytes and
inference. Large histories or unsupported swaps end in a coverage report.
Historical fiat valuation is an additional data source with an unquoted cost;
start in SOL/token units rather than invent dollar cost basis.

**Claims:** reconciled amounts and classified costs for supported transactions.
**Refuses:** tax-ready accounts, complete PnL over missing history, evidence that
Radar would have avoided a loss, or a causal assignment of residual loss to MEV.

**Screenshot:** “The difference between these recorded buys and sells is split
into supported fees, rent movements and an unreconciled remainder.”

**Why buy again:** review another session or month. Professionals may value the
record, but competitors already offer PnL. Radar must win on explanation and
reconciliation rather than on another balance chart.

### 5. Follow the evidence across launches

**Job:** investigate whether several launches share funding or transaction
participants, and show the precise links plus alternative explanations.

**Data and build:** launch blocks and creator history exist; historical
owner/funder resolution, directed fund flows and exclusions for routers,
exchanges and common infrastructure need additional work.
[0012](0012-recipient-sets-cannot-recur-authorities-can.md) explains the
token-account/owner distinction and ubiquitous-authority confound. Do not reuse
its old population counts as current ground truth. Adjacent transactions and a
common funder alone do not prove one actor, collusion or intent.

**Collection:** cap the investigation by seeds, hops, time window and calls—an
initial twenty seeds, two hops and 10,000 eligible raw reads is a proposed bound,
not proof it completes. The marginal credit equivalent is $0.05; raw-block bytes,
historical-account availability and local indexing can dominate. Use raw data
and local decoding, never vendor-parsed transactions. A limit reached is visible
partial evidence rather than “no connection.”

**Claims:** named transactions link specified addresses under stated semantics.
**Refuses:** identities, criminal intent, independent-person counts, complete
network discovery or token recommendations.

**Screenshot:** “These launches share this funding address; that links the
transactions, but it does not establish a common owner.”

**Why buy again:** investigate another cluster. Rank below the first four:
identity mistakes are expensive and free map products already cover part of it.
This direction is already in Radar's design material; it is not the novelty.

### 6. Design and run an edge investigation

**Job:** help a researcher formulate a falsifiable strategy, identify the data
it requires, preregister its comparison and report the result, including null
results. An advanced model may search for interactions the deterministic rule
missed. The deliverable is an experiment and evidence, not a trade signal sold
before validation.

**Data and build:** the feature and edge harnesses are substantial starting
assets. Missing production trade coverage and absent trustworthy labels must
remain visible. A customer-safe study workflow, model/version registry, retained
attempt history and fresh evaluation datasets are new. This is not the
interactive backtest optimiser GOAL rejects: users do not tune repeatedly on a
revealed holdout and receive the best-looking historical strategy.

**Collection:** shared launch/outcome history plus explicitly acquired granular
trades and coverage provenance. Bound each experiment by rows, time span,
CPU/RAM, raw bytes, model calls and external reads. At the checked raw tariff,
one million one-credit calls represent $5, but that is not an all-in collection
estimate. Whole-venue streams can dominate memory, bandwidth and retention.
**End-to-end dollar cost is unknown until a bounded pilot is measured**; this
note invents no production dataset or free compute budget.

**Claims:** a specified protocol was run on a named dataset/version and returned
the reported result. **Refuses:** profitable deployment, “AI alpha,” proof from
an unrun harness, or simulated returns described as realised trading.

**Screenshot:** “This hypothesis did not clear its registered net-return test;
the report also shows the launches whose outcomes we could not measure.”

**Why buy again:** a professional researcher may buy the next reproducible study.
For ordinary traders the willingness to pay for a null result is especially
uncertain. Fund the first edge discovery work as Radar R&D; do not make customers
finance an indefinite promise that profitable trading is coming.

## Ranking by value divided by cost to build

**These are decision estimates, not measurements or delivery promises.** Value
is an ordinal 1–5 judgment of recurring usefulness to the stated first audience,
discounted for evidence and demand uncertainty. Cost is a rough range of
person-weeks from this baseline to an instrumented private pilot, including the
necessary data/customer workflow but excluding legal procurement and a public
launch. Ratios use range midpoints to expose the reasoning, not its precision.
Shared foundations overlap; the rows must not be added into a project schedule.

| Rank | Capability | Value / 5 | Build range; midpoint | Value / midpoint | Reason |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | Claim investigation and size/cost worksheet | 5 | 3–5 weeks; 4 | 1.25 | Frequent new-token job; strongest reuse of real dossier data. Must beat free output on the specific question. |
| 2 | Saved-case watches and evidence changes | 5 | 5–9; 7 | 0.71 | Clearest recurring service. Recovery, coverage and useful notification selection cost more than polling. |
| 3 | Prospective caller audit | 4 | 6–10; 8 | 0.50 | Distinctive question for this audience; timing and exit evidence make it materially harder. |
| 4 | Personal execution-cost autopsy | 4 | 7–13; 10 | 0.40 | Concrete money accounting and recurring review, offset by cross-venue decoding/reconciliation burden. |
| 5 | Cross-launch funding investigation | 4 | 8–16; 12 | 0.33 | Useful deeper research; confounds, data coverage and free substitutes weaken the ratio. |
| 6 | Customer-facing edge investigation | 3 | 10–18; 14 | 0.21 | Largest conditional upside, least reliable near-term paid deliverable. Current instrument limits make cost uncertain. |

The first two form one credible initial product. Do not build six products
before asking for a second payment. Rank 6 is a **commercial launch order**, not
an instruction to stop strategy R&D until five other products ship. Run bounded
internal edge research alongside demand discovery when resources permit.

The order is not stable across audiences. A professional with many external
calls could put rank 3 first; a frequent trader with accounting pain could prefer
rank 4. For a casual degen seeking immediate entry signals, none may beat a free
tool. If all they value is an unproven profit prediction, there is no honest
paid offer for that use case today.

## The financial model and the actual cost boundary

### Charge for managed work, with transparent research credits

**Recommendation:** free local software and standard deterministic evidence;
optional prepaid credits for bounded managed investigations and watches. Treat
the free hosted service as rate-limited shared infrastructure, with its limits
visible. This proposes a commercial boundary; it does not assert that today's
gated routes or accepted billing ADRs already implement it.

| Model Josh raised | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Credit packages | Start here. Episodic research fits them; a second purchase tests value. Price completed scopes or metered service units with an upfront maximum. |
| Monthly subscription | Add an opt-in monthly credit allowance once repeat use exists. It purchases continuing service and a defined budget, not unlimited inference. |
| Buy a year/full access | Later, if desired, annual prepayment for defined service and monthly allowances. Specify rollover, cancellation/refund terms and supplier-price exposure. It is financing and convenience, not a source of retention evidence. |
| AI tokens only, everything else free | Too narrow once the assistant collects data and watches cases. Either include those costs in the service rate or deliberately subsidise them within a hard budget. Never manufacture an LLM call merely to make a useful deterministic service billable. |
| Lifetime/unlimited access or trade-linked fees | Reject for the first offer. Unlimited variable-cost obligations are unpriced; turnover/performance fees create incentives and legal questions this research product need not introduce. |

A **test**, not a claimed optimal price: offer a $10 pack before an optional
$20/month replenishment. Keep the displayed balance in USD; if the UI needs
“credits,” 100 credits can mean $1 of Radar service. It does not mean $1 paid to
a model provider. Publish the retail debit rules and scope caps, with provider
cost and Radar's service margin distinguished. Credits are account entitlements,
not tradable tokens, investments, withdrawal balances or trading capital.

To make the trial offer reviewable, these are **hypothetical maximum retail
quotes**, not launched prices or measured task consumption:

| Bounded paid job | Example maximum debit | Resource envelope to validate |
|---|---:|---|
| Investigate one claim about one launch | $1 | Up to 1,000 eligible raw reads across approved passes and aggregate 20k input/2k output model tokens; source-linked case and unknowns. Ordinary deterministic facts remain free. |
| Deeper case with several follow-up checks | $5 | Up to 10,000 eligible raw reads and aggregate 200k/20k model tokens; no purchased social feed or unlimited graph expansion. |
| Watch up to five saved mints for 24 hours | $2 | A specified state read per minute and at most two model explanations within aggregate 40k/4k tokens; coverage, last state and stop notices remain readable. No promise to detect unsupported events. |

At those maximum debits, a $10 pack covers ten bounded cases, two deeper cases,
or six bounded cases plus two watch periods. The RPC/model examples below imply
external costs of $0.095, $0.95 and $0.216 respectively before other service
costs. These examples are useful only if the scope resolves real questions.
Instrument the complete cost and quality before offering them; permit fewer
resources or revise the price if they cannot meet both. No customer should
learn the scope limit only after paying.

This is capped work, not a $1 unlimited question: larger investigations get a
new quote. The maximum is reserved, with final debit under the disclosed tariff
and unused reservation released. A watch costs ongoing collection/operation
even when no model explanation is needed; make that separate charge visible.

Quote each job from a versioned service tariff with a margin floor and maximum
debit. Reserve that maximum, settle delivered work, release the remainder and
show an itemised receipt. No surprise overage or automatic refill by default.
Clearly label paid inconclusive research when it delivers the agreed evidence;
do not charge for an infrastructure failure presented as a completed answer.
The distinction needs a published refund policy and an internal failure budget.

Purchased balances should not depend on expiry to make the business work.
Prefer nonexpiring purchased credits and explicit refund/closure rules, subject
to the eventual legal terms. Cancellation stops future renewal; previously
purchased service and report access need clearly stated treatment. Automatic
top-up is a separate opt-in with a monthly ceiling.

For illustration, a case consuming 20k/2k model tokens plus 1,000 raw reads has
$0.095 external marginal cost at those rates. A deeper 200k/20k case with 10,000
reads costs $0.95. Adding 500 X posts makes the latter $3.45 before storage,
payments or support. A $1 flat “question” price can work for the first and lose
money on the others. A shared archive lowers repeat collection cost; it does
not make an unbounded investigation safe to price.

Use this management equation on **consumed service revenue**, not cash from
unused balances:

> Contribution = service revenue − model/data/compute/storage − payment fees
> − delivery/support/refund allowance. Operating profit also subtracts fixed
> engineering, legal, shared infrastructure and acquisition costs.

A proposed pilot target is at least **60% contribution** after those variable
service costs. For a fully consumed $20 domestic-card pack, with an assumed $2
delivery/support allowance, that leaves at most **$5.12** for model, data,
compute and storage: $20 − $0.88 − $2 − $12. The $2 is an assumption to measure,
not a support-cost finding. At $12 contribution per fully used pack, every
$1,200/month of fixed cost needs 100 such packs merely to break even. Actual
fixed cost and attainable volume are unknown.

This makes the business test explicit: can customers get enough useful work
from the balance while Radar meets that cost envelope? If not, raise the price,
narrow the scope or stop that offer. Do not count abandoned credits or token
price appreciation as proof of viable economics. An accountant should settle
revenue recognition, prepaid obligations, refunds and applicable sales/VAT
treatment before launch. Maintain cash to honour unused-service obligations.

The existing [Stripe entitlement ADR](../adr/0010-entitlement-is-read-from-stripe-never-recorded.md)
describes subscription access, not this consumable balance. Its no-webhook/no-local
subscription-copy decision does not by itself solve payment reconciliation or
credit usage. A future billing design must explicitly reconcile recoverable
payment facts with a durable record of delivered consumption. Do not pretend
the present daily question counter is that record.

## A lawful and professional commercial direction

**Credits versus subscription is a charging choice, not a financial-regulation
exemption.** The recommended launch posture is paid, read-only factual research
with no control over customer funds. That reduces several risks; it does not
establish an exemption for personalised analysis. This is a scoped primary-source
review checked 2026-09-08, not comprehensive current-law clearance.

| Question | Evidence and consequence for Radar |
|---|---|
| Does paid AI analysis make Radar an investment adviser? | The [SEC staff adviser overview](https://www.sec.gov/about/offices/oia/oia_investman/rplaze-042012.pdf) describes compensated securities advice/analysis and the conditional publisher exclusion. [Robo-adviser guidance](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2017-52) confirms algorithmic delivery does not remove advisory obligations. An on-demand answer about a particular token is not automatically general, impersonal publication. Counsel must review actual outputs and covered assets; “not financial advice” is insufficient by itself. |

Confirm entity, activities and required registrations with Tennessee-qualified counsel. |
| Is all spot-crypto research a CTA service? | The [CFTC definitions](https://www.cftc.gov/IndustryOversight/Intermediaries/index.htm) concern advice on specified commodity interests. No provider approval was obtained in this research. |

[Tennessee's enacted renewal amendment](https://wapp.capitol.tn.gov/apps/BillInfo/Default?BillNumber=SB1894&ga=113) adds state requirements. Verify applicable timing and checkout obligations; do not assume proposed subscription legislation is enacted. |

Operationally, separate **factual explanation** from **personalised trading
advice**. “At the size you entered, this captured state implies this impact” is
a calculation. “Given your savings and goals, put this much into this token” is
a different service. Portfolio recommendations, caller rankings designed to
induce copying, and execution deserve separate legal review even if a human
ultimately signs. Payment method does not cure misleading performance claims.

Use ordinary fiat service receipts. No initial performance fee, assets-based
charge, issuer-sponsored ranking or payment tied to trades. Preserve
[ADR 0013's token/prize decision](../adr/0013-a-community-token-exists-and-radar-holds-none-of-it.md):
creator fees go to prizes, so **they are not assistant revenue**. Zero holdings
and full payout do not remove every attention/volume conflict. Review that
association, public commentary and prize rules together; a research paywall
does not insulate the free public channel. This note neither reopens Josh's
settled X platform decision nor claims legal review has occurred.

The concrete pre-launch legal work is a scoped review of representative cases,
screens, billing terms, token/prize relationship and the key/control diagram.
Require decisions on allowed outputs, entity/registrations, marketing,
retention/privacy, taxes and customer countries. Maintain a **country ×
capability** list: research sales, personalised advice, execution, public
promotions and prizes have different boundaries. Start in reviewed markets,
enforce the list at sale/access and expand where demand justifies review. A
merchant of record could handle some billing/tax work; it does not confer
advisory or promotion authorisation. Legal and provider-onboarding costs are
unquoted fixed costs, not assumed to be free.

## How to prove the assistant deserves its claims and a second payment

### Product test before a large build

Recruit a proposed thirty eligible pilot users: ten active degens, ten
professional traders and ten researchers. The small sample is a learning test,
not a market estimate. These numbers and gates are recommendations chosen now,
not observed results. Recruitment and charging require a later authorised
launch; this task contacts nobody and takes no money.

First compare case outputs against the free-tool baseline on real questions,
blinding the review where practical. Record time to a supported answer,
unsupported assertions, material missing evidence, latency, every external cost
and human help. Then offer the stated $10 pack and let users decide whether
another case is worth it. Record the conversion denominator, including people
who decline; do not recruit only enthusiasts who have already agreed to pay.

Proposed continuation gates:

- At least fifteen of the thirty buy a first pack, and at least eight of those
  buyers return for another completed paid job within thirty days without
  a discount, prize or token reward. Also report voluntary second purchases,
  remaining balances and useful jobs delivered per dollar, by audience. A user
  with ample credits need not refill to demonstrate repeat use. Track renewal
  after comparable consumption; high credit burn must not manufacture retention.
  If fewer buy initially, do not silently reduce the hurdle or claim a precise rate.
- Median time to an independently supported answer improves by at least 30%
  against the baseline; every material financial assertion is traceable and
  uncertainty survives the summary. Log all corrections, not only successful
  examples. Missing data can be an honest result but must still be useful.
- Pilot contribution meets the proposed 60% target using actual delivered
  consumption and all variable costs. Track heavy users and p95 task cost,
  refunds, human support time and free-tier subsidy, not only averages.
- For watches, users mark at least half of delivered change notices useful and
  can see gaps/expiry. If notices arrive after they could matter, more model
  eloquence does not rescue the feature.

These thresholds are intentionally contestable. A failed gate should lead to
an explicit revision of segment, scope or price, followed by a fresh cohort.
Annual prepayment is not a substitute for renewal. A second credit purchase
could be one intensive weekend; check continued paid use over later months
before calling it retention. Require actual voluntary replenishment evidence
before scaling, extending observation when efficient service leaves balances
unspent. Do not count prize participation as demand for the assistant.

### The separate path to a demonstrated trading edge

Use the existing harness as a starting instrument, not a certificate. A model's
research suggestions need recorded hypotheses, allowed inputs, versions, sizing,
timing, exits, control selection and costs **before** evaluation. Record every
attempt. Fit on earlier data, purge overlapping horizons, embargo the boundary
and preserve untouched later windows. The current five-fold/three-fit-window
shape and minimum sample gates are useful mechanics; they do not solve all
dependence, multiple testing or missing-data bias.

Required additions to the evidence before a stronger claim:

1. Verify successful-event semantics and completed collection ranges. Freeze
   the population before labels, retain missing-label reasons and show coverage
   by window. Check that exclusion of unmeasurable exits does not manufacture
   profitable survivors.
2. Compare the advanced model against the deterministic strategy, a tradable
   matched control, a simpler model/tool workflow and no trade. Use equivalent
   timestamps, data access and budget; record differences rather than hiding
   them in the word “AI.”
3. Estimate net outcomes with executable entry/exit assumptions, declared
   latency and size. Use the applicable cost cohort; keep 850 bps for fresh
   launches unless new matched measurement supports a change. Charge a complete
   round trip once, with separately justified increments, and report sensitivity.
4. Use uncertainty that recognises clustered creators and time regimes, account
   for tried hypotheses, and require fresh prospective confirmation. The
   harness's `Found` label or a positive median alone is not deployment proof.
5. Keep research/shadow results separate from realised net returns. A positive
   outcome would justify a separate proposal for legally reviewed, explicitly
   authorised, tightly bounded live validation under the kernel and signer.
   This document grants none of that authority and leaves the policy closed.

A measured edge could eventually justify a much more valuable assistant. Its
economics must include data/model costs as well as trading friction, especially
at small positions. The first honest public improvement is a precisely scoped
prospective research result; a realised-performance claim needs realised data.

## Where this is weakest, and what would falsify the ranking

**Demand is the weakest part.** No Radar customers were interviewed or charged
for this note, and competitor list prices do not prove renewal. I infer that
traders will pay to reduce research labour; some mainly want fast tips or
reassurance. If free output answers their questions equally well, rank 1 falls
and the proposed first product has no paid advantage.

**The build estimates could be wrong by multiples.** They are source-informed
judgment, not engineering estimates approved by the implementer. Billing,
capture provenance, unsupported routes and reliable watches may dominate the
visible AI work. Before committing to a schedule, scope one end-to-end case and
measure its dependencies. If that cannot be made useful without broad ingestion,
the cheap first step assumed by the ranking disappears.

**Watching can fail on the timescale this audience cares about.** A technically
correct minute-old observation may be irrelevant in a launch that moves in
seconds. If most useful notices require infrastructure customers will not fund,
rank 2 should fall below a retrospective caller/cost audit. Do not relabel
delayed monitoring as protection.

**The novel caller audit has a difficult observation problem.** Submitted feeds
can omit losses, receipt time is not every follower's delivery time, and a price
is not an executable fill. If trustworthy prospective capture and exit evidence
are too costly, sell no “copyable return” metric. Its rank rises only if users
pay for the actual supported timing/audit result, not a more exciting mock-up.

**The legal perimeter is unresolved.** Factual, user-directed conversations can
slide into personalised advice. The US position is evolving, this review is not
a complete survey of subsequent enactments or challenges, and no country-wide
clearance, counsel opinion or processor acceptance exists here. Required
registration or restricted distribution can change both costs and demand.

**The data advantage is provisional.** Historical captures matter only if they
are correct, legally usable, recoverable and genuinely missing from cheaper
alternatives. Another service can copy the workflow or collect the same public
chain. No proprietary model, exclusive dataset or durable moat was established.

**The measurements constrain themselves too.** 0017's median does not prove
all strategies fail; 0024's one window does not fix a timeless threshold; 0020's
weak paths do not prove exits cannot improve. If a repaired, prospectively
tested assistant finds material net edge at executable size, the commercial
ranking should change substantially. Calling that impossible would repeat the
error design 0016 corrected.

The recommendation is specific: **build and price one evidence-backed
research workflow, test a second payment, and keep the edge programme alive.**
There is enough here for a credible paid pilot. There is not enough to advertise
profitable trading or to sell a year of unlimited future intelligence.
